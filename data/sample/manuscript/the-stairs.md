The lamp had not gone out in forty years. Elin climbed the stairs at dusk, as her father had, and counted every step. There were one hundred and twelve. On the sixtieth, the wind found the crack in the east window and sang through it, the same low note it had sung all her life.

At the top she trimmed the wick, wound the clockwork, and wrote the time in the log. Her father’s hand filled the pages before hers. His last line said only: *Fair. Wind north-west. T. came by.*

She had read it a hundred times. She did not know who T. was.
